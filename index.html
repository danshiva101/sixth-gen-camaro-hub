What made Gen Six different

A smaller, stiffer Camaro with sports-car priorities.

The sixth-generation Camaro moved from the heavier Zeta architecture to GM's Alpha platform, shared with Cadillac sport sedans but heavily reworked for Camaro. Chevrolet said more than 70 percent of the architecture was unique to this generation, with a lower mass structure and sharper responses.

That focus gave the car a broad personality: daily-drivable LT models, old-school LT1 and SS V8 trims, and ZL1 variants that could hold their own on road courses.

Model lineup

What each sixth-gen Camaro model brings.

This breaks the car down by the models people usually compare: LT, LT1, SS, ZL1, 1LE, and convertible body style.

LT

Balanced daily Camaro

1LT, 2LT, and 3LT focus on the V6 in 2024, with coupe or convertible bodies, standard 18-inch wheels, available 20-inch wheels, available Brembo front brakes, RS styling, and comfort upgrades on 2LT/3LT.

LT1

The V8 value play

LT1 keeps the simpler LT-style equipment set but adds the 455-hp 6.2L V8, standard 20-inch wheels, run-flat tires, front Brembo brakes, and performance suspension tuning.

1SS

The core muscle car

1SS adds stronger performance hardware around the LT1 V8: summer performance tires, front and rear Brembo brakes, enhanced cooling, rear spoiler, and an 8-inch display setup.

2SS

V8 plus premium gear

2SS keeps the SS performance setup and adds higher-feature interior and driver-assistance content similar to the 3LT, including comfort and visibility features.

ZL1

The supercharged flagship

ZL1 gets the 650-hp LT4 supercharged V8, 11 heat exchangers, wider summer-only tires, six-piston front Brembos, Magnetic Ride Control, dual-mode exhaust, Recaro seats, and available 1LE extreme track hardware.

1LE

The track package

Available on SS and ZL1 coupes, 1LE packages sharpen the Camaro with track-focused suspension, aero, cooling, wheel, and tire changes. ZL1 1LE goes hardest with 19-inch wheels and massive 305/325-width summer-only tires.

Packages and options

How the option list changes the car.

These are the packages and major options that shaped the sixth-gen Camaro, especially during the 2024 final model year.

RS Package

Appearance package for LT-style Camaros with darker lighting details, RS-specific grille treatment, black bowties, decklid lip spoiler, and available 20-inch wheel designs.

Redline Edition

Black-and-red visual theme with black exterior accents and red detail work. It gave the non-ZL1 cars a sharper street look without changing the whole powertrain setup.

Design Packages

Bundled wheels, badges, stripes, center caps, and interior/exterior trim pieces. These were the easiest way to make an LT, LT1, or SS look more custom from the factory.

Heavy-Duty Cooling and Brake Package

Available on lower trims to improve repeated hard driving with stronger cooling and braking hardware. It is the practical option before stepping all the way into SS or ZL1 territory.

Audio and Infotainment

Most lower trims use a standard 6-speaker audio system. The Bose premium system steps up the sound with 9 speakers in coupes or 7 speakers in convertibles, and it is tied to higher trims or the Technology Package depending on model.

1LE Track Performance Package

Track-focused SS/ZL1 package with more serious suspension, aero, braking, and tire behavior. The ZL1 1LE became the most extreme factory road-course version of the sixth-gen Camaro.

Collector's Edition

Final-year package for LT/RS, LT1, SS, and ZL1. LT/RS, LT1, and SS use Panther Black Metallic Tintcoat; ZL1 gets Panther Black Matte and was limited to 350 cars.

Garage 56 Edition

ZL1 special edition inspired by the Camaro NASCAR entry that raced at Le Mans, with unique graphics, aero dive planes, and rear-spoiler wicker bill details.

Convertible Top

Power soft-top body style available across LT, LT1, SS, and ZL1 models. The top adds open-air personality while keeping the same core Camaro layout.

Interior comparison

LT/LT1, SS, and ZL1 share the same cockpit shape, but the details move up fast.

LT and LT1 have a similar simpler cabin foundation, SS adds more premium and performance equipment, and ZL1 turns the cabin into the most focused factory setup.

Generation timeline

Nine model years of constant sharpening.

  1. 2016 Sixth generation launches

    New Alpha-platform coupe and convertible arrive with turbo-four, V6, and SS V8 power.

  2. 2017 ZL1 returns

    The supercharged LT4 brings 650 hp and turns the Camaro into a serious road-course weapon.

  3. 2018 ZL1 1LE pushes harder

    Aero, suspension, tire, and cooling changes create the most focused factory Gen Six variant.

  4. 2019 Mid-cycle refresh

    Updated front and rear styling, infotainment revisions, and lineup changes arrive.

  5. 2020 LT1 trim joins

    Chevrolet gives buyers a lower-cost path to the 455-hp V8.

  6. 2024 Final model year

    The lineup closes with LT, LT1, SS, ZL1, 1LE availability, and the Panther-inspired Collector's Edition.

2024 Collector's Edition

The final sixth-gen sendoff leaned into the Panther code name.

For the final year, Chevrolet offered a Collector's Edition on LT/RS, LT1, SS, and ZL1 trims. LT/RS, LT1, and SS cars received Panther Black Metallic Tintcoat with black accenting, while the ZL1 Collector's Edition used Panther Black Matte and was limited to 350 units.
